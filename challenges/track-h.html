Workforce & Labor Policy

Responding to automation, skills gaps, or job-quality disruption in the labor market

This challenge asks teams to design a policy response to a live labor-market disruption — automation/AI displacement, skills gaps, or job-quality issues — for a specific industry or region.

Challenge Document Join the Track H Discord Submit Your Project

Key Project Tasks

  1. Select a focus: AI/automation workforce transition, apprenticeship & vocational training expansion, gig-economy worker classification, unemployment insurance modernization, or occupational licensing reform.
  2. Ground the problem in labor-market data (employment, wage, displacement trends).
  3. Review existing policy levers (UI, WIOA funding, licensing law, labor law).
  4. Design a policy proposal with an implementation and funding plan.
  5. Identify which workers/regions are most exposed and how the policy addresses that.

Possible Approaches

  1. Retraining — design or expand a reskilling/apprenticeship pipeline funded through workforce boards.
  2. Safety-net — modernize unemployment insurance or wage insurance for displaced workers.
  3. Regulatory — reform occupational licensing or gig-worker classification rules.
  4. Employer-incentive — design tax or subsidy incentives for job creation/retention.

Required Deliverables

  • 4–6 page policy brief with labor-market data and recommendation
  • One-page fact sheet for a workforce board or state legislature audience
  • Slide deck (8–10 slides)
  • An implementation timeline showing funding sources and milestones
  • Process appendix (0.5 page)

Optional Advanced Tasks

  • Interview a displaced worker, employer, or workforce-board staffer (with consent)
  • Build a simple “who's exposed” map by industry/region
  • Cost-per-job-saved or cost-per-worker-retrained analysis
  • A dissenting-view memo from an employer or union perspective

Platforms & Tools

  • Data: BLS, O*NET, state workforce agency dashboards, Census Bureau
  • Legislative tracking: Congress.gov, state legislature sites
  • Drafting/collaboration: Google Docs, shared repo

Judging Criteria

CriterionWeight
Data-driven problem definition25%
Feasibility (political + fiscal)20%
Equity across affected workers/regions20%
Clarity of implementation plan20%
Presentation quality15%

Data & Privacy Requirements

  • Use only public, aggregate labor data — no individually identifiable worker records.
  • Consent and anonymization for any worker interviews unless attribution is agreed.
  • Disclose AI tool use.

Team & Deadline

  • Teams of 3–5; a member with HR, labor-economics, or workforce-board experience is a plus.
  • Suggested timeline: 3–4 weeks.
  • Final submission deadline: November 14, 2026.
