Economic & Fiscal Policy

Producing CBO/JCT-quality fiscal analysis for a live economic issue

This challenge asks teams to analyze a live fiscal or economic issue and produce budget-scoring-quality analysis for a legislative or executive-branch client — the kind of work CBO, JCT, or a state fiscal office would produce.

Challenge Document Join the Track D Discord Submit Your Project

Key Project Tasks

  1. Select a focus: tax policy reform, minimum wage/labor policy, housing affordability & zoning economics, trade/tariff impacts, public pension solvency, or small-business policy.
  2. Build a baseline using public economic data (BLS, BEA, Census, state revenue offices).
  3. Model at least two policy scenarios and their projected fiscal/economic effects.
  4. Identify distributional impacts (who gains, who pays, across income/region/demographic groups).
  5. Stress-test assumptions against a recession or high-inflation scenario.

Possible Approaches

  1. CBO-style scoring — a 10-year budgetary cost/revenue estimate with methodology notes.
  2. Distributional analysis — model impacts by income quintile, region, or industry.
  3. Comparative state — benchmark against states/countries that have tried the policy.
  4. Behavioral economics — incorporate likely behavioral responses into the model.

Required Deliverables

  • 4–6 page policy brief with baseline data, model outputs, and recommendation
  • Fiscal/economic model (spreadsheet) with clearly labeled assumptions
  • One-page scorecard summarizing costs, revenues, distributional effects
  • Slide deck (8–10 slides)
  • Process appendix (0.5 page)

Optional Advanced Tasks

  • Sensitivity analysis showing how results change under different assumptions
  • Simple interactive calculator (e.g., “estimate your household's impact”)
  • Comparative case study of a jurisdiction that implemented a similar policy
  • A dissenting-view memo arguing the strongest opposing case

Platforms & Tools

  • Data: BLS, BEA, Census Bureau, FRED, state revenue/budget offices, CBO/JCT public reports
  • Modeling: Excel/Sheets, Python (pandas/numpy), R
  • Legislative tracking: Congress.gov, state fiscal notes

Judging Criteria

CriterionWeight
Rigor & transparency of modeling assumptions25%
Data quality & sourcing20%
Distributional/equity analysis20%
Political & administrative feasibility20%
Clarity for non-economists15%

Data & Privacy Requirements

  • Use only public, aggregate economic data — no proprietary firm-level or individual tax data.
  • Disclose all modeling assumptions and data sources; no black-box models.
  • Do not present modeled projections as certainties — communicate ranges.

Team & Deadline

  • Teams of 3–5, at least one member comfortable with spreadsheet/statistical modeling.
  • Suggested timeline: 3–4 weeks.
  • Final submission deadline: November 14, 2026.
