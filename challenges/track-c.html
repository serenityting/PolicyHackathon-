Climate & Energy Policy

Balancing environmental, economic, and equity goals in a decarbonization or resilience policy

This challenge asks teams to design a decarbonization or climate-resilience policy that balances environmental, economic, and equity goals for a specific jurisdiction — city, state, or national.

Challenge Document Join the Track C Discord Submit Your Project

Key Project Tasks

  1. Select a focus: clean energy siting/permitting, grid reliability & AI data-center load, community-level decarbonization, climate finance/carbon markets, or climate adaptation/resilience.
  2. Establish the baseline (emissions, energy mix, or vulnerability data) for the chosen jurisdiction.
  3. Survey existing policy tools (incentives, mandates, carbon pricing, permitting reform).
  4. Propose a policy package with a phased implementation timeline.
  5. Model likely economic, environmental, and equity/community impacts.

Possible Approaches

  1. Permitting reform — streamlined siting rules with community-benefit safeguards.
  2. Market-based — a carbon pricing, cap-and-trade, or credit mechanism.
  3. Grid/tech — address AI-data-center load growth (rate design, interconnection reform).
  4. Community power — center a labor/union or frontline-community partnership model.

Required Deliverables

  • 4–6 page policy brief with baseline data and a phased implementation plan
  • One-page fact sheet suitable for public/media release
  • Slide deck (8–10 slides)
  • Simple quantitative model or estimate of emissions/cost impact
  • Process appendix (0.5 page)

Optional Advanced Tasks

  • Interactive chart/dashboard visualizing baseline vs. projected impact
  • Draft model legislative or regulatory text
  • Mini stakeholder mapping exercise
  • Scenario-test against a supply shock or extreme weather event

Platforms & Tools

  • Data: EIA, EPA GHG inventories, NREL data tools, state PUC filings
  • Modeling: Excel/Sheets, Python (pandas), or simple emissions calculators
  • Legislative tracking: Congress.gov, state legislature sites, Regulations.gov

Judging Criteria

CriterionWeight
Technical/scientific accuracy20%
Political & economic feasibility20%
Equity/community impact analysis20%
Quality of quantitative modeling20%

Data & Privacy Requirements

  • Use only public emissions/energy data — no proprietary utility data without explicit permission.
  • Consent and anonymization required for any community interviews.
  • Disclose AI tool use and modeling assumptions/limitations transparently.

Team & Deadline

  • Teams of 3–5, ideally with one member versed in energy/environmental data.
  • Suggested timeline: 3–4 weeks.
  • Final submission deadline: November 14, 2026.
