Science & R&D Funding Policy

Redesigning how science gets funded, who decides, and how success is measured

This challenge asks teams to redesign or defend a federal or state R&D funding mechanism — how science gets funded, who decides, and how success is measured.

Challenge Document Join the Track G Discord Submit Your Project

Key Project Tasks

  1. Select a focus: NSF/NIH/DOE grant-making reform, indirect-cost-rate policy, basic-vs-applied research balance, research security/foreign-influence rules, or state-level R&D tax credits.
  2. Map the current funding architecture (agency, appropriations process, peer-review structure).
  3. Identify a specific inefficiency, gap, or political vulnerability in that architecture.
  4. Design a reform with an implementation and transition plan.
  5. Assess impact on research output, workforce pipeline, and national competitiveness.

Possible Approaches

  1. Appropriations — a funding reallocation proposal with fiscal notes.
  2. Process-reform — redesign peer review, grant timelines, or overhead-cost rules.
  3. Competitiveness — benchmark against China/EU/UK science-funding models.
  4. Pipeline — center on early-career researcher funding and retention.

Required Deliverables

  • 4–6 page policy brief with baseline funding data and recommendation
  • One-page fact sheet for an appropriations subcommittee audience
  • Slide deck (8–10 slides)
  • A simple before/after funding-flow diagram
  • Process appendix (0.5 page)

Optional Advanced Tasks

  • Interview a researcher or grants administrator (with consent)
  • Comparative case study of another country's science-funding model
  • A researcher's-eye-view narrative of how the reform changes the grant-application experience
  • Draft model appropriations report language

Platforms & Tools

  • Data: NSF NCSES, NIH RePORTER, DOE Office of Science budget documents, AAAS R&D budget tracker
  • Legislative tracking: Congress.gov, agency budget justifications
  • Drafting/collaboration: Google Docs, shared repo

Judging Criteria

Data & Privacy Requirements

  • Public budget documents and aggregate statistics only — no confidential reviewer deliberations or unpublished proposals.
  • Consent and anonymization for any researcher interviews unless attribution is agreed.
  • Disclose AI tool use.

Team & Deadline

  • Teams of 3–5; a member with research/academic-grant experience is a plus.
  • Suggested timeline: 3–4 weeks.
  • Final submission deadline: November 14, 2026.
